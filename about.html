How we work

The job starts with somebody coming to look at it. Not a price down the phone, not a guess from a photograph on a message — someone standing in the garden working out how the tree comes down, where the waste goes and what is underneath it. That takes fifteen minutes and it is the only way to give you a number that does not change on the day.

After that it is simple. You get a fixed price for clearly described work. If you want to think about it, think about it — nobody will ring you three times that week. If you go ahead, we turn up when we said we would, do what was quoted, and take the mess with us.

What we will tell you

Some of this trade runs on people not knowing what they are looking at. We would rather be the ones who explain it:

  • When a tree does not need removing, and reduction or thinning would do the job for less money.
  • When the work needs council consent or six weeks' notice before it can legally start.
  • How far a hedge can be cut back before it stops growing back — particularly leylandii, which does not forgive it.
  • When a surface is too far gone for washing to improve, before you pay for washing it.
  • When the job is not ours. Roofs, drains and knotweed treatment all belong to somebody else, and we will say so.

Rubbish and where it goes

Everything we cut leaves with us and goes to a licensed site. Anyone removing waste from your property has to be registered with the Environment Agency as a waste carrier — and it is worth knowing that if unregistered contractors fly-tip your garden waste, the householder can be the one who gets prosecuted for it. Ask us for our registration number, and ask anybody else who quotes.

Safety

Tree work is genuinely dangerous, and it is dangerous for whoever is stood underneath as well as the person in the tree. Areas get cordoned, drop zones get kept clear, and if we ask you to keep the children and the dog inside for an hour, that is why. If a job cannot be done safely with the kit and the people we have, we will not attempt it — we will tell you what it actually needs.

Our qualifications and insurance

Two things worth knowing before anybody starts a saw in your garden:

  • We are insured. Ask to see the certificate before work starts — from us or from anybody else quoting. A contractor who will not show you theirs is telling you something.
  • The chainsaw work is ticketed. Cutting is done by an operator holding the NPTC / City & Guilds chainsaw certificates, not by somebody who bought a saw and had a go.

Neither of those is a small thing. An uninsured contractor who drops a limb through your conservatory leaves you to claim on your own policy, and an unticketed one climbing your tree is a problem you inherit.

Ring and ask for the specifics any time and we will tell you straight. Owner: send the exact chainsaw units held (CS30, CS31, CS38 and so on) and the public liability cover level, and both go on here in full — those are the two things customers actually check.

Our guarantee

If you are not happy with any part of the work, tell us before we leave — or ring us afterwards — and we will come back and put it right. Owner: confirm the wording and how long it runs for

What you can check yourself

You do not have to take anyone's word for any of it:

  • Waste carrier registration — search the Environment Agency's public register by name or registration number.
  • Insurance — ask to see the certificate. Anyone genuinely insured will show you without being asked twice.
  • Chainsaw tickets — ask to see the cards. They have names and unit numbers on them.

Anyone who gets funny about being asked has told you what you needed to know.

What matters to us

Four things we are not flexible about

Turning up

If we say a day, we mean it. If the weather stops us — and in Lancashire it does — you get a phone call, not silence.

The price

The quoted price is the price. It changes only if you ask for extra work, and then you are told what it costs before it is done.

Clearing up

Blown down, raked off, gates shut. A job is not finished because the cutting has stopped.

The law

Protected trees, nesting birds, waste carriage. We do not cut corners on any of it, and we will not be talked into it.

The area

Blackburn and beyond

We are based in Blackburn and most days we are somewhere within half an hour of it: Darwen, Accrington, Rishton, Great Harwood, Oswaldtwistle, up into the Ribble Valley around Whalley, Langho and Clitheroe, across to Burnley and Padiham, and down towards Chorley and Preston.

Working the same patch for a while has practical value. You learn which council covers which street for tree work, which roads you cannot get a chipper down, and which estates have shared access at the back and which do not.
